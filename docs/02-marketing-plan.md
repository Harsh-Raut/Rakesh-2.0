# PROJECT R — Marketing & Lead Generation Plan
**Objective:** a steady flow of qualified WhatsApp enquiries, and a booked-out wedding season.
**Not the objective:** followers.

---

## 1. Where DJ bookings actually come from

Ranked by conversion rate, best first. Most DJs spend their effort in exactly the reverse order.

| Rank | Source | Converts at | Effort to build |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Referrals from past clients** | 50–70% | Low — just ask |
| 2 | **Wedding planners & event agencies** | 40–60% | Medium — relationship work |
| 3 | **Venue &amp; banquet-hall recommendations** | 40%+ | Medium |
| 4 | **Google Business Profile / local search** | 20–30% | Medium — high intent |
| 5 | **Instagram** | 5–15% | High — but builds the proof everything else needs |
| 6 | **Wedding marketplaces** (WedMeGood, WeddingWire, Sulekha, JustDial) | 10–20% | Low, paid, noisy leads |
| 7 | **Paid ads** | 5–10% | Money, only worth it in season |

**Strategy in one line:** build the referral and planner engine first, use Instagram to manufacture the proof that makes those referrals easy, and use the website to close everything.

## 2. The funnel

```
SEE                →  CHECK                →  ASK               →  BOOK
Instagram Reels       Website               WhatsApp            Advance paid
Planner referral      Gallery + reviews     Same-day reply      Confirmation sent
Google search         Packages              Price range         Planning call
Venue recommendation  FAQ                                       
```

**The single most important number:** *time to first reply*. A DJ who replies in 10 minutes beats a better DJ who replies tomorrow. Everything else in this document is worth less than that.

## 3. Priority actions, in order

### Tier 1 — do these first (weeks 1–4, mostly free)
1. **Google Business Profile.** Create/claim it. Category "DJ". Add all service areas, real photos, WhatsApp link, hours. This is the highest-ROI free asset a local service business has.
2. **Get 10 reviews.** Message every past client individually with a direct link. Not a broadcast — a personal message naming their event.
3. **Website live** with the real number, real photos, real prices. Link it in every bio, every quote, every signature.
4. **Referral ask becomes routine.** 48 hours after every event: thank-you message + review link + "if you know anyone planning something, send them my way."
5. **List every past event** with date, type, venue and guest count. This becomes the gallery, the reviews, and the credibility in every future pitch.

### Tier 2 — the relationship engine (months 2–4)
6. **Wedding planners.** Identify 20 in your city. Meet or call — do not cold-DM. Offer a clear commission structure and a one-page rate card. Two good planners can fill a season.
7. **Banquet halls, farmhouses, hotels, resorts.** Get on their preferred-vendor list. Play one event there well and ask the manager directly.
8. **Venue nights.** One regular club or pub night keeps you visible, generates footage, and pays while it markets you.
9. **Photographers, decorators, caterers, MCs.** They sit at the same weddings. Refer each other. This network compounds.

### Tier 3 — the visibility layer (ongoing)
10. **Instagram Reels, 4–5/week.** Content spec in doc 03.
11. **Marketplace listings** — worth it only in wedding season, and only if you reply within minutes; those leads go to whoever answers first.
12. **Paid ads** — geo-targeted, in-season, ₹ capped. Never always-on.

| Channel | Job | Cadence | KPI |

## 5. Seasonality (plan the year around this)

| Period | Reality | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct–Feb** | Peak wedding season. Most of the year's revenue. | Take bookings, deliver, shoot everything. Raise rates on prime dates. |
| **Mar–Apr** | Tail end + college fests + corporate offsites | Chase fest committees and HR teams |
| **May–Jul** | Slow. Fewer weddings. | Build: shoot content, redo the site, meet planners, run club nights |
| **Aug–Sep** | Booking window for peak season | **Heaviest marketing push of the year.** Ads, planner meetings, Reels daily |
| **Dec** | Peak of peak + NYE | NYE is your single highest-value date. Sell it by October. |

**Rule:** the marketing effort for peak season happens in **August**, not December. By December you are working, not selling.

## 6. Pricing strategy

- **Show ranges, never silence.** "Pricing on request" loses the price-sensitive lead *and* the serious one. Bands filter the wrong leads out before they cost you time.
- **Three tiers, middle one flagged "most booked."** Most people pick the middle. Design the middle tier to be the one you want to sell.
- **Charge more for:** peak-season Saturdays, NYE, out-station travel, multi-day weddings, sub-4-hour "quick" bookings that still cost a full day of setup.
- **Never discount the rate — reduce the package instead.** Drop the LED wall, not the fee. Discounting teaches the market your price is fiction.
- **Advance is non-negotiable.** No advance, no date held. Say it kindly, hold it firmly.

## 7. Converting an enquiry (the actual script)

1. **Reply within the hour**, always. Even "Got it — checking, back to you in 20 minutes" counts.
2. **Confirm availability first.** That's the only thing they asked.
3. **Ask three questions:** venue/city, guest count, hours needed. That's enough to quote.
4. **Send a range, not a lecture.** "For a 400-guest reception in [city], 6 hours with full sound and lighting, it's usually ₹X–Y."
5. **Send proof unprompted** — two photos and a 30-second video from a similar event.
6. **Give one clear next step:** "Shall I hold the date for you?"
7. **Follow up twice**, at day 3 and day 7, then stop. Two follow-ups recover a surprising share of "we'll get back to you."

Most lost bookings are lost at steps 1 and 7.

## 9. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Slow replies during events | A second person on the phone during peak season; auto-reply that sets expectation |
| One bad review | Reply publicly, calmly, once. Then bury it under new reviews. |
| Over-reliance on one planner | Never let one source exceed 30% of bookings |
| Double-booked date | One calendar, single source of truth, updated the moment an advance lands |
| Equipment failure | Backup gear on site — already a published promise |
| Undercutting by cheaper DJs | Don't compete on rate; compete on setup, planning call and reliability |
