# PROJECT R — Brand & Positioning
**Purpose of this document:** define what the business sells, to whom, and why they should pick Project R over the other twelve DJs they're texting.

---

## 1. What the business actually is

Not "an artist with a fanbase." A **service business that sells nights** across four event types, where the buying decision is made by a stressed person comparing options on WhatsApp, usually on a phone, usually at night.

The entire brand exists to answer four questions fast:
1. Are they free on my date?
2. Do they do *my* kind of event?
3. What will this cost me, roughly?
4. Can I trust them not to ruin the night?

Everything on the site maps to one of those four. If a section doesn't, it gets cut — that's what happened to the first draft.

## 2. Positioning statement

> For anyone planning a night that has to go right — a wedding, a club evening, a company party — **Project R** is the DJ act that brings the full setup and reads the actual room, so nobody has to manage the music. Unlike a playlist operator, the set is planned with you beforehand and adjusted live.

**The one line:** *Book the night.*

**The promise underneath it:** you send a date, you get an answer the same day, and on the event day you don't think about the music once.

## 3. The four buyers

| Segment | Who's really deciding | What scares them | What closes them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Weddings & sangeet** | Bride/groom, siblings, or a wedding planner | "The dancefloor will be empty" / "The wrong song will play at the entry" | Photos of packed floors, planning call, entry-song control, dhol add-on |
| **Clubs, pubs & lounges** | Venue manager or promoter | "The room will empty by 11" / "unreliable" | Set samples, ability to work on the house rig, reliability |
| **Corporate & brand** | HR, admin or an event agency | "Unprofessional" / "explicit track in front of the MD" / "no invoice" | Run-sheet discipline, clean edits, GST invoice, contract |
| **Private & college** | Host, or a student committee | "Too expensive" / "won't take requests" | Clear pricing bands, compact setups, requests welcome |

**Design consequence:** the site never asks the visitor to identify themselves. All four paths are visible in the first scroll, and each service card carries its own WhatsApp message so the first chat already has context.

## 4. Differentiators (pick two, prove them everywhere)

1. **Same-day reply.** Most DJs lose bookings to slow replies, not to price. This is stated on the site, on the turntable badge, and in the FAQ — so it has to be true.
2. **Full setup included.** Sound, lighting, mics, backup gear and crew are in the price. The client doesn't have to source a sound company. This is what turns a rate comparison into a value comparison.
3. **Planning call before the event.** Must-play, do-not-play, entry cues, timings. Almost nobody at the mid-market does this, and it's the single biggest reason a wedding client relaxes.
4. **Backup gear on site.** Named explicitly, because "what if it stops" is the fear nobody says out loud.

## 5. Visual identity

Cyberpunk-club aesthetic — neon, dark, high-contrast — because it signals *this is a real nightlife professional*, not a hobbyist with a laptop. The system:

- **Palette:** Void `#04030a` · Cyan `#00f0ff` · Magenta `#ff2bd1` · Violet `#8b3bff` · Acid `#c6ff3d` · **Amber `#ffb43d`** · Alert `#ff2f4d` · WhatsApp Green `#25D366`
- **Colour meaning:** cyan/magenta = club energy. **Amber = weddings and warmth.** Green is reserved *exclusively* for WhatsApp CTAs so the eye learns "green = the way to get in touch."
- **Type:** Orbitron (headings) · Rajdhani (body) · Share Tech Mono (labels, data)
- **Shape language:** bevelled clipped corners throughout
- **Motion:** reveal on scroll, drifting grid, swaying lasers, live equalizer — all disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`

**Photography direction (most important asset you don't have yet):** shots must show *the crowd*, not the DJ. A packed floor from behind the console is worth more than any portrait. Every event needs: 3 crowd-wide shots, 1 rig/setup shot, 1 close-up of the console with lights, 1 vertical video of a peak moment.

## 6. Voice

Plain, direct, confident, no hype. Written like a competent professional texting you back.

**Say:** "Send me your date." · "Backup gear is on site." · "You'll see travel itemised, never as a surprise."
**Never:** "unforgettable memories", "we believe music is life", "world-class", "best DJ in the city".

The wedding market is drowning in flowery copy. Being blunt is the differentiator.

## 7. Guardrails

- **No fake numbers, no fake reviews.** Stats are blank placeholders (`000`) in the code and stay that way until real figures exist. Wedding clients call references.
- Prices shown as ranges/"starting from" only — never a firm number the site can't honour.
- Clean-edit commitment for weddings/corporate must actually be kept.
- Any claim on the site ("same-day reply", "backup gear") is an operational promise. Don't publish what you won't deliver.
- Photos of clients' events need their permission before going public.
